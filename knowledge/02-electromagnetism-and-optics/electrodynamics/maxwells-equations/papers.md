### 11. A reinterpretation of classical magnetism via the regular representation of displacement current
- **Authors:** Jin Jer Huang · **Year:** 2025 · **Venue:** arXiv preprint [physics.class-ph]
- **Links:** [arXiv:2511.14492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14492)

> The displacement current, introduced by Maxwell, has long caused confusion regarding its relationship to the magnetic field. To develop a new understanding of classical magnetism, this work first decomposes the displacement current into a localized internal component and an external field distribution by employing a discal regularization of the dipole distribution. Due to a surprising cancellation of the electric current by this internal displacement current, an integral expression for the magnetic field in terms of the external displacement current (or total current) enables a reinterpretation of classical magnetism. Consequently, the current equivalence is resolved and refined through this integral representation in both quasi-steady and quasi-static states. In the latter case, the nonzero external displacement current is exactly equal to---and can easily be mistaken for---the current in the circuit system. This paper proposes a potential solution to several longstanding historical disputes concerning the displacement current.

### 12. Kirchhoff`s Forgotten Contributions to Electromagnetism: Continuity Equation versus Displacement Currents
- **Authors:** Xavier Oriols, Robert Eisenberg, David K. Ferry · **Year:** 2025 · **Venue:** arXiv preprint [physics.hist-ph]
- **Links:** [arXiv:2512.05997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05997)

> In 1857, Kirchhoff published two seminal papers on the motion of electricity in wires. In that work, he was the first to derive what we now call the telegrapher`s equations, which describes the propagation of electromagnetic signals along a cable at the speed of light, in some conditions. How was Kirchhoff able to describe electromagnetic propagation as early as 1857, when the notion of displacement current which is believed to be the essential ingredient for the propagation of electric and magnetic fields was not introduced by Maxwell until 1861 and fully explained later in 1865? In this paper, we show that Kirchhoff was the first, in his 1857 paper, to introduce the continuity equation when discussing electromagnetic propagation. We argue that the continuity equation used by Kirchhoff is a more fundamental concept now as it was in 1865 than displacement current. In our view, the dynamics of charged particles can be formulated without invoking fields, but the dynamics of fields cannot be formulated without a continuity equation describing the properties of matter.

### 15. Maxwell's equations are universal for sparse linear algebra
- **Authors:** Yannick Augenstein · **Year:** 2026 · **Venue:** arXiv preprint [physics.optics]
- **Links:** [arXiv:2610.38913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38913)

> Optical and wave-based analog computing has re-emerged as a route to accelerating linear algebra, with metamaterials and photonic circuits shown to invert matrices and solve integral equations. However, it has remained unclear which problems such passive structures can represent, and at what cost, once encoding, precision, conditioning and readout are accounted for. Here, we show that every polynomially conditioned sparse linear system can be compiled into a three-dimensional dielectric -- a cavity filled with lossless, isotropic material of bounded permittivity -- whose time-harmonic response encodes its solution.
