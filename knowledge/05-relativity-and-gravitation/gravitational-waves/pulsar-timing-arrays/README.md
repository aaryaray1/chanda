# Pulsar timing arrays
> [Relativity and Gravitation](../../README.md) › [Gravitational waves](../README.md) · Literature: [papers.md](papers.md)

**Scope.** Millisecond pulsars as clocks, timing residuals, red noise and dispersion-measure variations, the Hellings–Downs angular correlation, NANOGrav, EPTA/InPTA, PPTA, CPTA, MeerKAT, IPTA combinations, supermassive-black-hole-binary backgrounds vs. cosmological sources (phase transitions, cosmic strings), continuous-wave searches.

**Core ideas.**
- A GW passing Earth shifts all pulse arrival times coherently; the correlation between pulsar pairs depends only on their angular separation (Hellings–Downs).
- Sensitive to $f\sim1$–100 nHz — orbital periods of years, i.e. supermassive binaries.
- 2023 results show the Hellings–Downs signature at 3–4σ; amplitude $A\sim2$–$3\times10^{-15}$ at 1/yr.

**Key equations.**
$$\Gamma(\zeta)=\frac32x\ln x-\frac x4+\frac12,\ x=\frac{1-\cos\zeta}{2};\qquad h_c(f)=A\left(\frac{f}{1\,\text{yr}^{-1}}\right)^{-2/3}$$

**Landmark.** NANOGrav (2023) — see [subfield landmarks](../README.md).
