# Neutron stars — where six branches meet
> Curated theme · [Connections atlas](../../CONNECTIONS.md) · Kind: shared object

**The bridge.** A neutron star is one object that needs nearly every branch:
- **Structure (GR):** hydrostatic balance follows the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff (TOV) equation, and the equation of state (EOS) fixes the mass–radius relation and the maximum mass.
- **EOS (nuclear):** near nuclear saturation density it comes from nuclear forces (chiral EFT).
- **Higher densities (particle):** hyperons, deconfined quarks and new-particle cooling channels may appear.
- **Crust (condensed matter):** a Coulomb crystal, possibly with "pasta" phases, alongside superfluid neutrons and superconducting protons.
- **Magnetosphere (plasma):** it drives pulsar emission.
- **Observation (astrophysics):** pulsar timing (e.g. two-solar-mass pulsars that force a stiff EOS) and gravitational waves. GW170817 measured tidal deformability, and its kilonova showed that mergers make r-process elements.

*Established framework; the EOS above about twice saturation density is current research.*

**Shared structure.**
$$\frac{dP}{dr}=-\frac{G\,[\rho+P/c^2]\,[m+4\pi r^3P/c^2]}{r^2\,[1-2Gm/(rc^2)]},\qquad \frac{dm}{dr}=4\pi r^2\rho,\qquad \Lambda=\frac23k_2\Big(\frac{Rc^2}{GM}\Big)^5$$
Here $\rho$ is the total mass-energy density divided by $c^2$, and $k_2$ is the tidal Love number. The EOS $P(\rho)$ is the hand-off between nuclear physics and GR, and $\Lambda$ is what GW detectors measure.

**Where it breaks.**
- Laboratory nuclear physics reaches only about saturation density with nearly symmetric matter. Neutron stars need several times that, with extreme neutron excess. In between there is extrapolation, not measurement.
- Whether there is a phase transition to quark matter, and whether it leaves an observable signature, is unknown.
- Superfluid gaps and crust properties are model-dependent, so glitch and cooling interpretations carry those uncertainties.
