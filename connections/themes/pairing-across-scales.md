# Pairing across scales — metals, nuclei, neutron stars and cold atoms
> Curated theme · [Connections atlas](../../CONNECTIONS.md) · Kind: mechanism

**The bridge.** Cooper's instability needs only three things: a sharp Fermi surface, any net attraction near it, and time-reversed partner states. So BCS theory is not specific to metals. A year after BCS, Bohr, Mottelson and Pines pointed to the nuclear analogue. In even–even nuclei:
- the spectrum has a gap;
- binding energies stagger between odd and even nucleon number;
- moments of inertia are well below the rigid-body value.

The same physics predicts neutron superfluidity and proton superconductivity inside neutron stars. In ultracold Fermi gases a Feshbach resonance tunes the attraction continuously from BCS pairs to a BEC of tightly bound molecules. *Established; neutron-star gaps are current research.*

**How each branch sees it.**
- **Condensed matter:** [BCS theory](../../knowledge/08-condensed-matter/superconductivity/bcs-theory-and-conventional-superconductors/README.md). Phonon-mediated attraction gives $\Delta\sim$ 0.1–1 meV; for Al, $\Delta\approx0.18$ meV.
- **Nuclear physics:** [Shell model and collective models](../../knowledge/07-nuclear-physics/nuclear-structure/shell-model-and-collective-models/README.md). The empirical gap is $\Delta\approx12/\sqrt A$ MeV, about 1–2 MeV. Pairing shapes the drip lines in [exotic nuclei](../../knowledge/07-nuclear-physics/nuclear-structure/exotic-nuclei-and-drip-lines/README.md).
- **Nuclear astrophysics:** [Neutron-star equation of state](../../knowledge/07-nuclear-physics/nuclear-astrophysics/neutron-star-equation-of-state/README.md). The crust and core host $^1S_0$ neutron, $^3P_2$ neutron and proton pairing, which bears on cooling and glitches.
- **Astrophysics:** [Compact objects](../../knowledge/09-astrophysics-and-cosmology/stellar-astrophysics/compact-objects/README.md).
- **AMO physics:** [BECs and degenerate gases](../../knowledge/04-quantum-physics/atomic-molecular-and-optical-physics/bose-einstein-condensates-and-degenerate-gases/README.md). Tuning the BEC–BCS crossover and reaching unitarity gives a universal strongly interacting Fermi gas. Dilute neutron matter is close to that regime.

**Shared structure.**
$$\Delta_{\mathbf k}=-\sum_{\mathbf k'}V_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}\,\frac{\Delta_{\mathbf k'}}{2E_{\mathbf k'}}\tanh\frac{E_{\mathbf k'}}{2k_BT},\qquad E_{\mathbf k}=\sqrt{\xi_{\mathbf k}^2+|\Delta_{\mathbf k}|^2},\qquad \Delta(0)\approx1.764\,k_BT_c\ \text{(weak coupling)}$$

**Where it breaks.**
- *Nuclei are small.* The pair coherence length exceeds the nuclear radius, so there is no sharp transition. Particle-number fluctuations matter, which calls for number projection.
- *Neutron-star gaps are model-dependent.* The $^3P_2$ gap is especially uncertain because it depends on in-medium interactions.
- *The crossover is smooth.* Cold-atom BEC–BCS has no phase transition in between, and unitarity has no small parameter.
